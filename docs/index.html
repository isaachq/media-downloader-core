New in beta 22

Five more sites read by the app itself, Twitch live channels recorded and watched, and one list that says what each site downloads.

Five new sites

Threads, Bluesky, Twitch, SoundCloud and Snapchat: posts, carousels, videos, tracks and whole public profiles, read by the app itself — with the same checks as every other download.

Record Twitch live

Paste a channel that is on the air and press Record: it goes on until you stop it, the stream ends or 12 hours pass, with the ads left out. Stop and save keeps it; Discard throws it away.

Watch while it records

Watch opens a player on the stream, alone or beside a recording. Closing the player never stops the recording.

Twitch videos and SoundCloud, faster

Past broadcasts, highlights and SoundCloud tracks come down piece by piece, several at once, joined without re-encoding: a one-hour 1080p60 video took about seven and a half minutes, checked frame by frame.

Which sites, and what they need

One list says what each site downloads, whether it needs your sign-in and whether the Android app has it — on this page, in the app and under every link you paste.

Sign in for Threads and Twitch

Private Threads profiles you follow and Twitch’s subscriber-only videos open with the session of the browser you already use. No password is ever asked.

Made for the big jobs

Most downloaders handle one video at a time. This one is built for a whole playlist, a whole profile, a whole search — and for leaving it running while you do something else.

Whole playlists and profiles

YouTube playlists, channels and Mixes; whole profiles of TikTok, Instagram, Facebook, Threads, Bluesky, Snapchat and SoundCloud, carousels included (as a video, or as images with their sound); Twitch videos and live channels; X and VK — with “Load all” for the thousands of posts of a veteran account.

Pick before you download

A preview lists everything a link holds, with length and publication date. Filter, select all or just a few — only what you pick is downloaded. Or gather links of any sites in a mix list: one batch, with the format you choose for each.

Every file verified

Each download is checked — codecs, duration, every frame decoded — and its SHA-256 is recorded. A broken file is downloaded again; one broken at the source is marked and never downloaded twice.

Everything finished, in one place

The Finished tab keeps every file of every session, batch by batch: search it, play it, show it in its folder, check it again, export the list. If something failed, one click retries it — and you see why it failed.

A live report of the whole run

What is pending, downloading, being verified and finished — per batch, with time left, refreshed every 15 seconds. Copy it as text with one click.

Knows your disk and your time

Before it starts, a batch tells you how much space it needs. While it runs, the time left is measured from your computer's real speed — start a big batch and walk away.

Plays in your car and on your TV

MP4 in H.264/AAC, MP3 with the tags car stereos read, and file names with plain letters and numbers that every USB stick accepts. Or M4A, Opus, FLAC, WAV, WebM, MKV.

Sorted by when it was published

Each file carries the date it was published, so sorting a folder by date sorts your backup by publication — not by the minute it happened to download.

Keeps itself up to date

New versions arrive inside the app: one button downloads them, checks them against GitHub's SHA-256, installs and restarts. Its tools (yt-dlp, FFmpeg) set themselves up the same way.

Sync Up: accounts that stay current

Point a TikTok account at its folder — even one that already has its videos — and each sync brings only what is missing, in the folder's own format. Every day at a set time, every few hours, or when you say.

Tells you when it is done

Leave it working in the background: a system notification arrives when a batch ends, and the Dock or taskbar icon shows how far along it is.

Also a command line and a library

The same engine runs from a terminal (mdc) and from your own code — schedule account syncs with cron, launchd or Task Scheduler, no app open.

Three steps

No command line, no settings to learn first — the first time, a short guide shows you around. In fourteen languages, light or dark.

Paste a link

A video, a playlist, a channel, a profile of any supported site, a live Twitch channel — or a whole list of links of any sites.

Pick what you want

Everything it holds is listed first. Choose MP4 or MP3, the quality and the folder.

Let it work

Several downloads at once, each one verified. Watch the report, or come back later.

See it

Private content: sign in on your browser

Private or members-only videos, age-restricted ones, Instagram profiles and stories, a Facebook page’s videos — and YouTube when it asks to confirm you’re not a bot — need your own account. The app never asks for a password: it uses the session of the browser you already sign in with.

Sign in on your browser

Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari or Brave — to Google / YouTube, Instagram or Facebook, as always.

Choose it in the app

Settings → Cookies from browser → that same browser. “How to sign in” shows these steps.

Allow it once

Your system asks for permission the first time (see below for yours).

Paste the link again

It downloads with your session. Signed out? Sign in again on the browser.

macOS

A Keychain window asks to let the app read “Chrome Safe Storage” (or your browser’s): type your Mac password and click Always Allow. Safari: System Settings → Privacy & Security → Full Disk Access → turn on Media Download Core.

Windows

Close the browser before downloading — Chrome and Edge lock their cookies while open. If Chrome still can’t be read (it protects its cookies on Windows), sign in with Firefox and choose Firefox.

Linux

On Fedora, Ubuntu and others, your keyring (GNOME Keyring / KWallet) may ask to allow access to the browser’s saved data: allow it. Firefox asks nothing.

Your cookies stay on your computer: read once into a private copy used only for downloading, and never sent anywhere else.

Download

The newest version is always on GitHub Releases.

macOS

  • Apple Silicon (M1–M4).dmg
  • Intel.dmg

First launch: System Settings → Privacy & Security → “Open Anyway” (once). It isn't signed with a paid Apple certificate yet.

No “Open Anyway” button? Drag the app to Applications and run this once in Terminal:

xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/Applications/Media Download Core.app"

Windows

  • 64-bit (most PCs).exe
  • ARM64.exe
  1. Open the .exe. If a blue window says “Windows protected your PC”, click More info.
  2. Click Run anyway. The installer opens, finishes by itself and starts the app.

Why the warning? This is a free project made at home, and it isn't signed with a paid Microsoft certificate yet. The warning is about that missing signature, not about the file — its code is public on GitHub, and Windows stops asking as more people install it.
